# Zero-Cash Revenue Engine

## What this is

A **zero-upfront-cost** operating plan for selling a productized lead-recovery service to local home-service companies (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, appliance repair). The service is intentionally manual-first: sell the outcome before buying software, use the client's existing tools wherever possible, and only automate after a client has paid.

**Target:** $30,000/month in contribution profit, which averages $1,000/day over a 30-day month. This is a target, not a guarantee. It requires sales, delivery, retention, taxes, and lawful outreach; those are not automatic.

## Primary offer

**CallbackOps — Missed-Lead Recovery:** respond to missed calls and web inquiries, qualify the request, and book or route the next step using the client's phone/CRM/calendar. Start with a spreadsheet + client-owned inbox/phone tools. Do not buy ads or software before a client pays.

### Revenue math

- Founding offer: $1,500 setup after the first verified result + $1,500/month for the first three clients.
- Standard offer: $2,500/month for the recurring service; client pays any paid messaging/CRM tools directly.
- $12 clients x $2,500/month = $30,000/month contribution before taxes and the operator's labor.
- A safer pricing path is to raise new-client pricing after the first three clients have documented proof.

## Operating constraints

1. Operator cash invested before the first client payment: **$0**.
2. No paid ads, purchased lead lists, or paid automation tools.
3. Use personalized, permission-respecting outreach; do not bulk-spam or impersonate businesses.
4. Do not promise a specific number of jobs or revenue. Promise a process and a measurement window.
5. Client owns customer data and accounts. Get written permission before touching them.
6. Any paid tool is either client-owned/client-paid or added only after cash is collected.
7. All secondary lanes (competitions, bounties, paid tasks) are opportunistic; they do not replace the primary sales engine.

## First 7 days

### Day 1 — Set the offer up

- Replace `YOUR_NAME`, `YOUR_EMAIL`, and `YOUR_CALENDAR_LINK` in the landing page and scripts.
- Pick one metro and one niche (default: HVAC or plumbing).
- Create a free business email and a one-page public profile only if you do not already have them.
- Build a list of 30 businesses from public websites/Google Maps. Record only business-public contact details and the exact issue observed.

### Day 2 — Build proof without pretending

- Create two sample audits from public information. Mark them as examples; do not claim they are client results.
- Practice a 10-minute call: current lead flow, missed-call handling, booking process, and average job value.
- Send 10 highly personalized messages, not 100 generic ones.

### Day 3 — Sell a paid pilot

- Send 10 more personalized messages and make 5 polite calls during business hours.
- Offer a 14-day pilot with a defined baseline and success measure. Ask for payment for setup only after the agreed verification event if that reduces friction.
- Do not activate on a client's production phone or CRM until written authorization exists.

### Day 4 — Close and onboard

- Collect payment or a signed order form before doing custom implementation.
- Get the client's written authorization, hours, service area, exclusions, calendar rules, escalation contact, and privacy requirements.
- Start with a shared sheet and a client-owned inbox/calendar; keep the workflow auditable.

### Day 5 — Deliver and measure

- Log every inbound lead, response time, status, outcome, and next action.
- Send a daily summary. Escalate emergencies, medical/safety issues, angry customers, and anything outside the script.

### Day 6 — Turn delivery into proof

- Ask for permission to use anonymized numbers only after the pilot. Capture the baseline, work performed, and verified outcome.
- Convert the pilot to recurring service if the client agrees the process is valuable.

### Day 7 — Repeat and raise quality

- Contact 20 more good-fit prospects, using a different personalized observation for each.
- Review reply rate, calls booked, show rate, and close rate. Fix the bottleneck instead of blindly increasing volume.

## What can and cannot be automated

- Safe to automate later: reminders, spreadsheet status updates, templated drafts, daily reports.
- Keep human review for: customer promises, emergency triage, refunds, disputes, sensitive data, and anything that can create legal or safety exposure.
- Do not build a speculative SaaS product before someone pays for the service.

## Honest path to $1,000/day

The shortest plausible path is recurring B2B revenue, not prize hunting. Competitions and agent tasks can provide cash, proof, or leads, but their availability and payout timing are uncertain. Use them as a secondary lane while building the 12-client base.

See `opportunity-lanes.md` for the screening rules.
