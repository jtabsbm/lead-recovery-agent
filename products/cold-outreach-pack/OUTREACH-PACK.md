# Cold Outreach Pack for Home Services

**B2B email templates, follow-up sequences, phone scripts, and prospecting rules for selling services to home-service contractors — personalized for five trades**

James Thompson · CallbackOps · First Edition

---

## Read This First (3 minutes)

This pack is adapted from the working outreach SOP of a real lead-recovery service currently prospecting home-service businesses — not generated filler. It contains everything we send, in the order we send it, plus the rules that keep it legal, welcome, and effective.

**What this pack is:** a complete cold-outreach system for selling B2B services (lead recovery, marketing, bookkeeping, software, consulting — anything an owner buys) to HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and garage-door companies.

**What this pack is not:** a spam kit. Every rule below exists because unpersonalized bulk outreach gets you ignored at best and reported at best-worse. The whole method is: find one true thing, say it, offer an exit.

### The five rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Public information only.** Everything you personalize with comes from the business's own website or public listing. No scraped private data, no purchased lists of dubious legality.
2. **One observable detail per message.** Not "I love your company." A specific, factual observation: your service area page lists La Mesa but not El Cajon; your last Google review mentions a 3-day callback wait; your homepage offers 24/7 service but the form says "we'll reply within 48 hours."
3. **Never pretend.** No posing as a customer, employee, vendor, or "I was in your shop yesterday." Faked familiarity is the fastest route to a deleted email and a bad reputation in a tight-knit trade community.
4. **Respect the exit and the opt-out.** Every sequence ends cleanly by touch 3. Keep a suppression list. Honor opt-outs immediately and permanently. Know your local email/telemarketing rules (CAN-SPAM in the US, CASL in Canada, GDPR in the EU — this pack is a template, not legal advice; you're responsible for compliance in your jurisdiction).
5. **Volume is the last lever, not the first.** If replies are low, fix the observation and niche fit before increasing volume. 10 excellent messages beat 100 mediocre ones — and keep your domain out of trouble.

### How to use this pack

1. Pick your trade (Sections 1–5).
2. Build your prospect list using the field spec in Section 6.
3. Send the initial message, follow up per the sequence, stop at the close-out.
4. Track everything, tune per Section 8's diagnostics.

**Merge fields used throughout:** `{NAME}` owner/manager name if publicly listed · `{BUSINESS}` business name · `{METRO}` metro area · `{NICHE}` trade · `{OBSERVATION}` your one true thing · `{YOUR_NAME}` your name · `{YOUR_EMAIL}` your email.

## The Universal Sequence (all trades)

Three touches, then stop. More touches on cold B2B to busy owners has diminishing returns and reputation cost.

| Touch | When | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Initial | Day 0 | Email or contact form | Observation + offer + easy exit |
| 2 — Follow-up | Day 2 (business days) | Email | One more reason: the sample audit |
| 3 — Close-out | Day 7 | Email | Last note, door open, stop |

If they reply at any point: stop the sequence immediately and have a human conversation.

### Touch 1 — Initial (universal)

**Subject:** quick missed-call idea for {BUSINESS}

> Hi {NAME},
>
> I was looking at {BUSINESS} while researching {METRO} {NICHE} companies. I noticed {OBSERVATION}.
>
> I build a small missed-lead recovery workflow: when a call or web inquiry is missed, the lead is logged, the approved response is sent, and the next step is booked or routed to your team. It can start with your existing tools and human review — no ads and no software purchase required to test the process.
>
> Would a 10-minute review of your current missed-call flow be useful? If it is not relevant, reply "no" and I will not follow up.
>
> {YOUR_NAME}
> {YOUR_EMAIL}

### Touch 2 — Follow-up (Day 2, universal)

> Hi {NAME},
>
> Following up once on the missed-call workflow idea. The only reason I reached out is {OBSERVATION}. I can send a one-page sample audit showing exactly what I would measure, with no access to your systems.
>
> If this is not a priority, just say so and I will close the loop.
>
> {YOUR_NAME}

### Touch 3 — Close-out (Day 7, universal)

> Hi {NAME},
>
> Last note from me. I am closing this out unless missed-call recovery is worth discussing. If you want the sample audit later, reply "audit." Otherwise, no response needed.
>
> {YOUR_NAME}

### Phone opener (universal)

If you call instead of (or alongside) email:

> "Hi, this is {YOUR_NAME}. I help local {NICHE} companies recover missed calls and web inquiries. I am not calling to sell ads. Could I ask who handles missed-call follow-up, and whether a short workflow audit would be useful?"

If the owner is unavailable:

> "Thanks — what is the best public way to send a two-minute overview? I will keep it to one message."

## 1. HVAC

**Why they listen:** no-heat/no-AC calls are emergencies that cannot wait for a callback; summer and winter peaks create exactly the missed-call storms this service fixes.

**What to observe (find one, use one):**

- "Your homepage promises 24/7 emergency service, but your contact form confirmation says replies within 48 hours — those two promises fight each other on your busiest nights."
- "Your Google reviews mention great work but a couple mention it took a couple of days to get a callback — that's usually a capacity problem, not a caring problem."
- "You list {SUBURB} in your service area but I didn't see it mentioned in any of your recent reviews — wondering if those leads reach you at all."

**Touch 1 — Initial:**

**Subject:** 48-hour form reply vs. your 24/7 promise, {BUSINESS}

> Hi {NAME},
>
> I was researching {METRO} HVAC companies and {BUSINESS} caught my eye: your site promises 24/7 emergency service, but the web form confirmation says you'll reply within 48 hours. On a no-AC night in August, that gap is where those customers call someone else.
>
> I build missed-lead recovery for HVAC shops: every missed call and after-hours form gets logged, a fast approved response goes out, and emergencies escalate to your on-call tech immediately. Starts with your existing tools and human review — nothing to buy to test it.
>
> Worth a 10-minute look at your missed-call flow? If it's not relevant, reply "no" and I won't follow up.
>
> {YOUR_NAME} · {YOUR_EMAIL}

**The 3-touch sequence:** Initial → Day 2 follow-up (use universal Touch 2 with the HVAC observation) → Day 7 close-out (universal).

**Trade-specific objection handling:**

- *"We answer every call during business hours."* → "That's rarer than you'd think — nice work. My lane is what happens at 9pm in July: the after-hours forms, the second call you can't take while under a unit. Does anyone see those before morning?"
- *"We have an answering service."* → "Great first layer. What I add is the layer after: when the message lands, who logs it, responds, and books it — and what the escalation path is for a no-heat with a baby in the house. Want to compare notes on the handoff?"

## 2. Plumbing

**Why they listen:** bursts and backups are midnight emergencies; water damage multiplies by the hour, so speed is the whole product — and price-shopper quotes for water heaters go quiet constantly.

**What to observe:**

- "Your site lists emergency service, but there's no text option — and most burst-pipe customers at 11pm are texting, not emailing."
- "Your reviews are strong on work quality; a few mention waiting a day-plus for quote follow-ups on water heater replacements."
- "You publish your service area as {LIST}, but your form doesn't ask for address — so out-of-area leads eat your team's time before you find out."

**Touch 1 — Initial:**

**Subject:** your 11pm burst-pipe leads, {BUSINESS}

> Hi {NAME},
>
> I was researching {METRO} plumbing companies and noticed {BUSINESS} offers emergency service but no text-back option for after-hours leads. From what I see, most 11pm burst-pipe customers are texting, not filling forms — and whoever answers that text first usually gets the job.
>
> I build missed-lead recovery for plumbing shops: missed calls and after-hours messages get logged, an approved fast response goes out, emergencies escalate to your on-call plumber, and quote requests get a structured follow-up so water-heater bids stop going quiet.
>
> Open to a 10-minute review of the after-hours flow? If it's not relevant, reply "no" and that's the end of it.
>
> {YOUR_NAME} · {YOUR_EMAIL}

**Sequence:** Initial → Day 2 universal follow-up → Day 7 universal close-out.

**Objection handling:**

- *"Our phones forward to the owner's cell at night."* → "Works until it doesn't — vacation, dead zone, the second call of the night. Recovery is the backup for the backup: logged, responded to, escalated. What happens tonight if the forward misses one?"
- *"We're slammed, we don't need more leads."* → "This isn't more leads — it's the ones you already have. The ones that rang while you were under a sink. Recovering those costs zero ad spend."

## 3. Electrical

**Why they listen:** panel upgrades and EV charger installs are high-ticket, considered purchases — exactly the quotes that go quiet for a week and get decided without you; and burning-smell calls are safety escalations that cannot sit in an inbox.

**What to observe:**

- "You're ranking well for EV charger installs in {METRO}, but there's no quote follow-up process visible — that's a considered purchase where the second contact usually wins."
- "Your site has an emergency line, but the web form buries it below fold — a sparking-outlet customer is not scrolling."
- "Several of your recent reviews mention getting answers fast — you're clearly good on the phone. What happens to the form leads at 8pm?"

**Touch 1 — Initial:**

**Subject:** your EV-install quotes going quiet, {BUSINESS}

> Hi {NAME},
>
> I was researching {METRO} electrical contractors and noticed {BUSINESS} shows up strong for EV charger installs — a high-ticket job where customers collect 2–3 bids and decide over days. From your site I can't see any structured follow-up on those bids, and in my experience the second contact usually wins a considered purchase.
>
> I build missed-lead recovery for electrical shops: every inbound is logged and classified, burning-smell/spark emergencies escalate to a human immediately, and big-ticket quotes get a polite 5-touch follow-up over 14 days so the decision doesn't happen without you.
>
> Worth 10 minutes to see the flow? Reply "no" if not relevant and I won't follow up.
>
> {YOUR_NAME} · {YOUR_EMAIL}

**Sequence:** Initial → Day 2 universal follow-up → Day 7 universal close-out.

**Objection handling:**

- *"Our estimators handle follow-up."* → "When they're not on job sites. The cadence runs whether the estimator's day blew up or not — and logs every touch so nobody guesses who contacted whom."
- *"Sounds like another CRM."* → "It's a spreadsheet and a phone, deliberately. No logins, no migration. If it earns its keep in 14 days, then we talk tools."

## 4. Roofing

**Why they listen:** roofs are infrequent, high-stakes purchases customers research heavily; multiple-bid collection is the norm; and insurance-claim leads have deadlines and documentation needs that punish slow, sloppy intake.

**What to observe:**

- "Your portfolio is strong, but there's no visible process for following up on inspection requests — roof bids are a 2–3 estimate race."
- "You advertise storm-response, but your form doesn't mention insurance — the first company to ask about the adjuster usually frames the whole claim."
- "Half your recent reviews mention 'fast response' — that's your brand. What's the plan for keeping it true during storm season volume?"

**Touch 1 — Initial:**

**Subject:** the 3-bid race on roof bids, {BUSINESS}

> Hi {NAME},
>
> I was researching {METRO} roofing companies and noticed {BUSINESS} has a strong portfolio but no visible follow-up process on inspection requests. Roofing is a 2–3 estimate race, and homeowners usually go with whoever followed up — not necessarily the best bid.
>
> I build missed-lead recovery for roofers: inspection requests get an immediate response with two concrete time slots, insurance-mention leads get asked for claim number and adjuster contact right away, and every open quote gets a 5-touch follow-up over 14 days.
>
> Would a 10-minute look at your intake flow be useful? If not, reply "no" and I'll close the file.
>
> {YOUR_NAME} · {YOUR_EMAIL}

**Sequence:** Initial → Day 2 universal follow-up → Day 7 universal close-out.

**Objection handling:**

- *"Storm season we answer everything; off-season it's slow."* → "Then the ROI question is off-season: the bids you're not following up on now are next spring's jobs. The cadence keeps working when the phones go quiet."
- *"We work off referrals, don't need it."* → "Referral leads are the highest-intent ones you get — the exact ones you least want ringing out while you're on a roof. Recovery protects the referral channel you already built."

>
> {YOUR_NAME} · {YOUR_EMAIL}

## 6. Prospect List & Tracker Fields

Track every prospect in a sheet or CSV with these fields — this is the exact field spec from our working pipeline:

```
business name | metro | niche | public website | public phone |
public email or contact-form URL | owner/manager name if publicly listed |
observation (specific and factual) | likely lead-recovery gap |
first contact date | channel | response | next step | opt-out flag
```

Rules:

- The **observation** field is filled in *before* first contact, from public research. No observation, no send.
- **Opt-out flag** set the moment anyone says no. Check it before every send.
- The **response** and **next step** fields are what make follow-ups honest — you'll know exactly where each prospect stands without memory.

**Finding the observation (10 minutes per prospect, top):**

1. Load their website. Homepage, services page, service-area page, contact page.
2. Check their Google Business profile reviews (last 10) for callback/wait mentions.
3. Write ONE factual sentence. If you can't find one true specific thing, skip the prospect — generic outreach is worse than no outreach.

## 7. The Sample Audit (your conversion asset)

When a prospect replies "maybe" or "what is it," don't send a brochure. Send the one-page sample audit — it's the highest-converting asset we have because it shows the work instead of claiming it:

> **Business:** {BUSINESS}
>
> **Public observation:** {FACTUAL_OBSERVATION}
>
> **Possible leak:** {MISSED_CALL_OR_INTAKE_RISK}
>
> **Pilot measurement:**
> - inbound calls/inquiries
> - missed or unanswered items
> - time to first response
> - qualified conversations
> - booked appointments
> - unresolved items
>
> **Proposed first change:** {ONE_SMALL_WORKFLOW_CHANGE}
>
> **Boundary:** this is a sample based on public information; it is not a claim about your internal operations.

That last line matters. The audit is labeled, bounded, and honest — which is exactly why owners read the whole page.

## 8. Daily Cadence & Diagnostics

### The daily rhythm

- 10 new personalized prospects contacted
- 5 follow-ups sent
- 5 live-call attempts during appropriate business hours
- 1 short audit or role-play delivered
- Update the pipeline before you stop for the day

### Diagnostics: read your reply rate like a dashboard

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low replies (<5%) | Weak observations, wrong niche, or bad fit | Improve observation quality; narrow the niche; don't raise volume |
| Replies but no calls booked | The offer is unclear or the CTA is too big | Sharpen to one sentence; make the ask 10 minutes, not a meeting |
| Calls booked but no closes | Discovery call problem | Fix the call script (see the Playbook's ten discovery questions) |
| "Not interested" > 50% of replies | Targeting the wrong businesses | Your list-building criteria, not your template, is the problem |
| Bounces > ~30% on info@ addresses | Unverified emails | Find a real address or use the contact form (see Section 9) |

### The tuning loop

Weekly: read the diagnostics table, pick ONE problem, fix it in the templates or the list criteria, run another week. Outreach improves by iteration on real replies, not by template-swapping in the abstract.

## 9. Bounce Recovery & Contact-Form Protocol

Roughly a third of info@domain.com addresses bounce. When one does:

1. **Re-check the site** for a real contact email: homepage, /contact, /contact-us, /contactus. Look for `mailto:` links and plain-text addresses.
2. **If a contact form exists** — use it with the personalized message. One submission, not three. Many are Wix/React-built; if it fails once, note it and move on (don't hammer it).
3. **If neither exists** — some sites legitimately have only a phone number. Mark BLOCKED and move on. This is a volume business; don't romanticize one prospect.
4. **Log the outcome** (bounced / form-submitted / blocked) in the tracker with the contact method used.

**Never:** guess email formats (first@, firstlast@) at scale without verification; buy email lists; ignore an opt-out; re-contact a bounced address with a "did you get this?" nudge.

## 10. Compliance Notes (read once, remember always)

This pack is a template, not legal advice. You are responsible for compliance with the rules of your jurisdiction and the recipient's jurisdiction:

- **US (CAN-SPAM):** don't use deceptive subjects/headers; identify yourself; include a way to opt out; honor opt-outs promptly; include your physical address if required for your mail setup.
- **Canada (CASL):** much stricter — commercial electronic messages generally require consent (express or, with limits, implied). B2B implied consent has specific conditions and expiry; research before sending Canadian cold email.
- **EU/UK (GDPR/PECR):** legitimate-interest bases for B2B cold email are narrow and contested; get advice before EU/UK outreach.
- **Telemarketing:** the universal phone script is for live, one-to-one B2B calls during business hours; check the US (TSR/TCPA), Canada (DNCL), and your local equivalents for rules on recorded lines, calling times, and do-not-call obligations.
- **General:** one touch sequence per business per campaign; suppression lists permanent; never remove someone who opted out.
